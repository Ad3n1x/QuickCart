# QuickCart — DPO, major-importance registration and audit assessment

**Status:** Assessment required. No registration, exemption or compliance certification is claimed.

## Major-importance assessment

NDPA section 65 and NDPC GAID 2025 / Schedule 7 must be applied to the real processing operation. The guidance includes thresholds and designated classes; relevant indicators include:
- Processing personal data of more than 200 data subjects in six months.
- Providing commercial ICT services on digital devices that store personal data belonging to another individual.
- Processing in specified sectors/classes designated by NDPC.
- Other factors including sensitivity, financial data entrusted, reliance on cloud infrastructure, cross-border flows, automation and number of data subjects.

QuickCart provides digital storefront/marketplace functionality to sellers and processes buyer/seller information. Whether it meets the commercial-ICT criterion or another class must be assessed against actual product terms, business model, volumes and current NDPC guidance. Do not conclude it is outside scope solely because it is early-stage, free at launch or operated by an individual.

## DPO

GAID Article 7 and Article 11 require a DPO for controllers/processors of major importance. The DPO may be internal or provided under a service contract, but must have appropriate competence, independence, access and resources. DPO contact details must be published and communicated to NDPC as prescribed.

No DPO is currently appointed. Do not publish a fabricated DPO contact or imply that the privacy mailbox is a DPO.

## Audit and filing

GAID Article 7 expects controllers/processors to conduct compliance audits within 15 months of commencement of business and annually thereafter. Major-importance categories have additional registration and Compliance Audit Return requirements. Confirm category, deadlines, fees and filing route against current NDPC guidance and portal.

## Evidence pack

Actual data-subject counts per rolling six-month period; business model and seller terms; sector/special-category analysis; ROPA and data flows; DPIA and LIA where required; vendor DPAs and transfer assessments; security, retention, incident, DSAR and child-data procedures; private controller/operator details; DPO credentials and service contract if applicable.
